# Ballade of the Granite Kings — notes

**Share URL:** https://compose.grepawk.com/s/ballade-of-the-granite-kings-ue6z96tj

Narrative ballade in D Dorian, 4/2, quarter = 120, 39 bars, ~2:40. First ballade, first Dorian piece and first 4/2 meter in the series. Written for the new low-register rule: every tune sits in cello, horn, viola, clarinet or trombone range (top note E5), with contrabasses and tuba an octave under the bass down to C1.

Orchestra: clarinet, bassoons, horns, trombones, tuba, timpani, harp (low), choir, violas, strings, cellos, contrabasses. No flute, violin solo, trumpet or glockenspiel.

Form: horn call over a D pedal, cello ballad, horns with a cello counterline, F major episode through B-flat and E-flat to A, low-brass climax, coda from G minor and A7 to D major.

## Drafts and critique

1. **Ballade of the Granite Kings, D Dorian, 4/2.** Hook: singable but narrow (D–B-flat); opening bar too stepwise. Harmony: 0 checker issues, Dorian G major and E-flat colour, Picardy ending. Form: clear story arc, best of the three. Groove: slow, broad, a little static in the two theme statements. Style: cinematic and new for the catalog. Low warmth: strong (low/high gap 23.8 dB).
2. **Berceuse for the Sleeping Mountain, A-flat major, 3/4.** Hook: gentle falling C–B-flat–A-flat, plain. Harmony: 0 issues, borrowed D-flat minor in the coda. Form: tidy ABA, 1:55. Groove: rocking harp, soothing but flat dynamically. Style: more chamber lullaby than cinematic. Low warmth: good (24.2 dB gap).
3. **The Undertow, E Phrygian, 4/4.** Hook: strong Phrygian B–E–F cell. Harmony: 3 parallel octaves left, A Phrygian development. Form: driving, 1:47. Groove: best drive (3+3+2 low-string ostinato). Style: cinematic, but too close to Tidebreaker and Caravan of the Glass Dunes. Low warmth: best (25.2 dB gap).

**Winner: draft 1.** Fullest form and the most new ground for the catalog, with no voice-leading faults.
