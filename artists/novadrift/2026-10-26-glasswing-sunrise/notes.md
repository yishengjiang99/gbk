# Creative notes — Glasswing Sunrise (Nova Drift)

- Concept: the morning after yesterday's night drive (Neon Tidepool); light coming up over the freeway, hopeful and weightless.
- Key/tempo: Eb major with a Lydian A-natural lift, 76 BPM, 4/4, 52 bars (~2:48 including the fade). Yesterday was F# minor, 92 BPM, nocturnal.
- Progression: Ebmaj9 – Cm9 – Abmaj7(#11) – Bbsus4; Glasswing turns to Abmaj9 – Fm9 – Cm7 – Gm7/Bb.
- Structure: First Light (1–12, pads and glass bells, half-time arp from bar 9) → Glide (13–28, 16th saw arp, 8th synth bass,
  soft drums, lead melody from bar 21) → Glasswing (29–40, drums out, 8th-note arp, high quiet melody fragments) →
  Sunrise (41–52, fullest point with a voice pad and the melody up an octave, slow fade from bar 49).
- Sounds (GM / GeneralUser GS): Warm Pad 89, Halo Pad 94, Saw Lead 81 (arp + echo), Synth Bass 38, Crystal 98 (lead), Voice Pad 91, GM drums.
- v1 listen-back: the first 4 bars sat at -59 dB RMS, about 30 dB under Glide, so the song started with near-silence. Sunrise was
  no louder than Glide (-28 vs -29 dB), there was no climax, the fade fell off abruptly in the last bar, and the saw lead was harsh.
- v2: glass-bell (Crystal) lead with intro bells and Glasswing fragments, a dotted-8th echo track on the arp, 5-note arp voicings,
  octave-bounce bass, claps layered on snare, kick pickups, hat risers into each drop, higher intro pad expression and velocity.
  Re-analysis: intro was -43 dB (better), but the fade now hit -70 dB by bar 52 and left ~10 s of silence.
- v3 (published): Voice Pad (91) added under Sunrise, and pad/halo fades stop at a floor instead of going to zero, so the fade is gradual
  (-34 → -45 → -51 dB over the last bars). The master was first loudnorm'd in dynamic mode, which flattened the arc (intro raised to -20 dB);
  I replaced that with a linear +11.5 dB gain into a gentle limiter.
  Final master: -16.3 LUFS, peak -1.7 dBFS, LRA 12 LU, no clipped samples, 168 s, MP3 192k. Section RMS: -28 / -17.5 / -22 / -17 dB, then the fade.
- All material is original, from the seeded generator in compose.py. It doesn't quote any existing melody.
